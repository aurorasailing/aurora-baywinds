Albert Park Lake — the rules

Parks Victoria runs Albert Park Lake. The rules below come straight from the state Vessel Operating and Zoning Rules — Schedule 151, the part written for this lake. They're in plain language here; the VOZR is the authority.

On the water

No engines on the lake151.2 · Cl 10

The whole lake is closed to powered vessels — the only exception is a boat authorised by Parks Victoria and on its approved-vessels list. So a club safety or rescue boat with a motor can only be out there if Parks Victoria has approved it and listed it.

Authorised powerboats: 5 knots151.1 · Cl 7

Any engine-driven vessel that is allowed on the lake is held to five knots — a slow, no-wash pace.

No vessels at night151.3(a) · Cl 9

No vessel may be on the water from one hour after sunset until two hours before sunrise. An evening sail has to be off the water within the hour after sunset — plan the pack-up around that, not the dark.

Some craft aren't allowed151.3(b) · Cl 9

These are prohibited on the lake: multi-hulled sailing boats (catamarans), sailboards (windsurfers) and kiteboards. Single-hull dinghies — what we sail — are fine.

"Stop No Vessels" areas151.4 · Cl 9

Where "Stop No Vessels" marker buoys are set out, all vessels must stay out — the whole time the buoys are in place. They mark events, works, or protected water such as around Gunn Island. If you see them, keep clear.

The lake is set aside for vessels151.5 · Cl 13

The whole lake is a special-purpose area for the exclusive use of vessels — the sailing and rowing craft that use it. That's the footing we sail on.

The general Victorian rules still apply too. On top of Schedule 151, the state-wide rules hold — most of all, keep to 5 knots within 50 metres of a person in the water, the water's edge, a structure, or another vessel. These are in the same VOZR.
