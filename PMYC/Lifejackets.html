Lifejackets

Victoria's rules, in plain language, for how we sail. PMYC is on Port Phillip Bay, which the regulations class as enclosed waters, and our boats are small (dinghies and safety boats, 4.8 m or under) — so a lifejacket must be worn, not just carried.

What we need at PMYC

On enclosed waters (Port Phillip Bay), worn at all times our boats are afloat:

Anyone under 12
Level 100, worn at all times, on any boat — sail or power. The master on board is responsible for making sure every child under 12 is wearing one.
12 and over — dinghy off-the-beach yacht
Level 100 or Level 50, worn. (The RS Quest and X3 are "off-the-beach sailing yachts".)
12 and over — safety / power boat ≤ 4.8 m
Level 100, worn.
Power boat 4.8–12 m
Level 100, worn at times of heightened risk (see below); otherwise carried for each person.
Children come first. A child under 12 always needs a Level 100 — that's stricter than the "Level 100 or 50" allowed for adults on a dinghy. Fit matters: crotch straps are crucial for children so the jacket can't slip off.

The three levels

Level 100+ (Type 1)

100 newtons or more. Turns the wearer onto their back and holds a safe floating position; collar supports the head; high-visibility, often with light and whistle. The jacket for open or rougher water. Crotch straps strongly recommended. Not ideal where you may hit the water at speed.

Level 50 (Type 2)

Less buoyancy; does not turn you onto your back, but keeps you afloat. High-visibility. Allows more movement — used for dinghy sailing, kayaking, and craft where you might hit the water at speed.

Level 50S (Type 3)

Like a Level 50 but without the high-visibility features (often dark). Only suitable when help is close at hand. Not accepted for our enclosed-waters use above — listed only for completeness.

Heightened risk

In these circumstances, a lifejacket must be worn even where it would otherwise only need to be carried (this makes the 4.8–12 m power boat a "worn" situation, and our under-4.8 m boats are already "worn"):

  • Only one person on board (sailing or boating alone) — and a sole adult with children under 12 counts as boating alone.
  • From one hour after sunset to one hour before sunrise.
  • A current Bureau of Meteorology warning — gale, storm-force or hurricane-force wind, severe thunderstorm, or severe weather.
  • Restricted visibility; a disabled vessel.
  • A yacht with no lifelines, rails, harnesses or jacklines in use.
  • Any significant likelihood the boat may capsize or swamp, or people may go overboard — which, honestly, is normal dinghy sailing.
  • Port Phillip Heads (within 3 nm of Point Lonsdale) is a designated hazardous area — all vessels under 12 m must wear one there.

Carry vs wear

You must carry an appropriate, correctly-sized lifejacket for every person on every vessel. On top of that you must wear one on any boat 4.8 m or under, and at times of heightened risk — which covers everything we sail. There must be enough jackets for everyone aboard, kept in good condition.

Good to know

  • Infants: Safe Transport Victoria recommends not taking infants on a recreational boat.
  • Inflatables are not lifejackets until inflated — service them per the maker's instructions and check the cylinder.
  • Foam jackets — check fastenings, wash down after use, store dry to avoid mould and deterioration.
  • Standard change: from July 2028, AS 4758.1:2022 will be the only accepted standard — AS 1512 and ISO 12402 will no longer be accepted. Worth checking our fleet's jackets against that date.

Source: Safe Transport Victoria — Wear a lifejacket, current at October 2026, and the Marine Safety Regulations (Vic).
